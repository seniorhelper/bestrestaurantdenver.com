The Best Brunch
In Denver

Not our opinion. We counted how many credible Denver publications independently recommend each place, and sorted by that. The methodology is on the page.

Where is the best brunch in Denver?

By cross-source consensus, Safta in RiNo is the strongest pick — it is the only Denver brunch that also carries Michelin recognition, and it runs an all-you-can-eat weekend spread. Beyond that, Onefold, Root Down, Lucile's, Sam's No. 3, Snooze and Mimosas all appear repeatedly across Westword, 5280 and Time Out. Denver has no single consensus winner, which is itself informative.

Denver takes brunch more seriously than almost any city its size. There is a reason for that, and it is not just the mimosas.

This is an outdoor city with a lot of people who were up at five to get to a trailhead. So brunch here is frequently the first real meal of the day rather than a leisurely late one. Consequently, the good places open early, fill fast, and are busiest between nine and eleven rather than at noon.

Now, the harder problem: every brunch list on the internet is somebody's opinion, and most of them are affiliate-driven. Therefore we did something different.

How this list was built

We Did Not Pick These.
We Counted Them.

The method, in four steps

  1. We chose the sources first. Westword, 5280, Time Out Denver and the MICHELIN Guide Colorado. Two are Denver's established alt-weekly and city magazine. One is a national title with a Denver desk. One is an inspection body.
  2. We pulled their published brunch recommendations as of 2026, without adding anything of our own.
  3. We counted independent mentions. A restaurant appearing in three of those sources scores three. Michelin recognition counts as a source and is flagged separately, because Michelin does not rate brunch specifically.
  4. We sorted by that count. Ties break alphabetically. No payment, no affiliate links, no placement fees.

What this measures: how much editorial agreement exists about a restaurant. What it does not measure: whether you personally will like it. Those are different questions, and conflating them is how most “best of” lists go wrong.

The interesting finding is that Denver has no runaway consensus brunch. In a city like New Orleans or Charleston, one or two names appear on every list. Here, the recommendations spread across a dozen places in a dozen neighborhoods.

That is a sign of depth, not indecision. It means the good brunch near you is probably genuinely good, so you do not need to drive across town for it.

The local move

Order It Smothered

What is a smothered breakfast burrito?

A breakfast burrito buried under Colorado green chile — a pork-based stew of roasted green chiles, thinner than the New Mexico style and meant to be poured. It is the single most Colorado thing you can order at brunch, it usually costs a dollar or two extra, and most out-of-state visitors never think to ask for it.

Here is the thing nobody tells visitors. In Denver, "smothered" is a verb you apply to almost anything on a breakfast menu.

Burritos, obviously. But also eggs, hash browns, biscuits, an omelette, a burger at eleven in the morning. Ask, and it usually happens.

The chile itself is typically made from Pueblo chiles — often the Mosco variety, which has thicker walls and more heat than a standard Hatch-type pepper. We go deep on why that matters in our guide to what to eat in Denver.

Three brunch orders that are actually from here

  • A smothered breakfast burrito. Eggs, potato, cheese, a protein, then green chile poured over the top and melted under a salamander. This is the dish.
  • A Denver omelette, smothered. Ham, onion, green pepper, cheese. Ordinary on its own. Transformed by chile.
  • Green chile as a side. Order a cup by itself. Then put it on everything else that arrives. Nobody will comment.

One seasonal note. Roasting season runs roughly August through October, peaking in September. Kitchens that buy fresh and freeze their own chile are noticeably better in autumn and early winter than they are in June. Consequently, a September brunch in Denver is a genuinely different meal from a June one.

Practical

How To Actually
Get A Table

Do you need reservations for brunch in Denver?

For most Denver brunch, no — but you will wait. Popular spots run 45 to 90 minute waits between 9:30 and 11:30 on weekends. The exceptions are the higher-end and buffet-style brunches, which do take reservations and do sell out. Going before 9 a.m. or after 1 p.m. removes almost all of the pain.

Go early. Genuinely early.

Denver wakes up early because a large share of the city is trying to be at a trailhead by seven. As a result the brunch rush starts sooner here than in most cities. An 8:30 arrival is usually a walk-in. A 10:30 arrival is frequently an hour.

Or go late

Many Denver brunches run until two or three in the afternoon. A 1:15 table is quiet, unhurried and often gets better service, because the kitchen is past the crush.

Weekdays exist

Several of the places on this list serve brunch every day rather than only at weekends. Meanwhile, a Wednesday brunch is essentially a private dining experience by comparison. If your schedule allows it, take it.

Understand the bottomless rules

Colorado law places limits on how bottomless a bottomless mimosa can be, and restaurants handle it differently. Most cap the window at ninety minutes or two hours, require a food order, and will not serve a table where anyone is over the line. So read the fine print before you build a plan around it, and do not argue with the server — the rule is not theirs.

Patios change the calculus

Denver averages a lot of sun, so patio brunch is viable well into October and often on random warm days in winter. Still, patio seats are usually first-come rather than reservable, and everyone else has the same idea on the first warm Saturday in March.

Check before you drive

This one matters more than usual right now. Denver's restaurant scene has turned over hard — several established rooms closed in mid-2026 alone. Therefore, a two-minute check of the restaurant's own page before you leave the house is worth more than any list, including this one.

By neighborhood

Where To Brunch,
By Where You Are

Because nobody drives across Denver for eggs. Pick the one near you.

RiNo

Safta on Brighton Boulevard is the standout, and it is the only Denver brunch on this list that also carries Michelin recognition. It runs weekend mornings only, so plan ahead. Otherwise RiNo is dense enough that walking two blocks usually solves the wait problem.

LoHi and the Highlands

The heaviest concentration of well-regarded brunch in the city. Root Down occupies a converted auto garage with gardens on site. Bar Dough does an Italian-leaning weekend brunch. Sassafras handles the classic American version on West 32nd. Meanwhile the Bindery covers the bakery-and-cafe end of the spectrum.

Uptown and Five Points

Onefold's original Uptown location opened in 2015 and built its reputation on making eggs interesting again — bacon fried rice, congee, breakfast tacos. Mimosas on Welton Street leans Southern and does exactly what its name promises.

Downtown and Ballpark

Sam's No. 3 is the Denver diner institution, and it is downtown. The Lobby in Ballpark has taken Westword recognition for its bottomless drink deal several years running. Both are more about the room than the plate, which is sometimes the correct priority.

Wash Park and south

Lucile's has been doing Cajun and Creole in West Wash Park for decades, and the beignets are the reason. Expect to wait. Expect it to be worth it.

Everywhere else

Snooze started in Denver and has multiple locations across the metro, so there is probably one near you. Acova in the Highlands has an on-site playground, which makes it the answer if you are travelling with children. Star Kitchen on the southwest side does dim sum, which is brunch by any reasonable definition.

Denver Dining, Answered

Where is the best brunch in Denver?

By cross-source consensus, Safta in RiNo is the strongest pick — it is the only Denver brunch that also carries Michelin recognition, and it runs an all-you-can-eat weekend spread on Saturdays and Sundays. Beyond that, Onefold, Root Down, Lucile's and Sam's No. 3 all appear repeatedly across Westword, 5280 and Time Out. Denver has no single runaway consensus winner, which is a sign of depth.

Do you need reservations for brunch in Denver?

For most Denver brunch, no — but you will wait. Popular spots run 45 to 90 minute waits between 9:30 and 11:30 on weekends. The exceptions are higher-end and buffet-style brunches, which do take reservations and do sell out. Arriving before 9 a.m. or after 1 p.m. removes almost all of the pain.

It usually costs a dollar or two extra and it is the single most Colorado thing you can order at brunch. In Denver you can ask for almost anything on a breakfast menu smothered.

What time does brunch start in Denver?

Earlier than in most cities. Many Denver brunch spots open at 7 or 8 a.m., and the rush starts sooner here because a large share of the city is trying to reach a trailhead by seven. An 8:30 arrival is usually a walk-in. A 10:30 arrival on a Saturday is frequently an hour's wait.

Are bottomless mimosas legal in Colorado?

Bottomless mimosa service exists in Denver but is limited by state alcohol rules, and restaurants handle it differently. Most cap the window at ninety minutes or two hours, require a food order alongside it, and will not continue serving a table where anyone is visibly over the line. Read the restaurant's terms before you build a plan around it.

What neighborhood is best for brunch in Denver?

LoHi and the Highlands have the heaviest concentration of well-regarded brunch, including Root Down, Bar Dough, Sassafras and the Bindery. RiNo has Safta, the only Michelin-recognised brunch on this list. Uptown and Five Points have Onefold and Mimosas. Snooze has locations across the metro, so there is usually one near wherever you are staying.

Is Denver a good brunch city?

Yes, and unusually so for its size. This is an outdoor city where many people are up before dawn, so brunch is often the first real meal of the day rather than a late leisurely one. What is the best brunch in Denver for families?

Acova in the Highland neighborhood, which has an on-site playground and a serious gluten-free commitment. Snooze locations are also reliably family-friendly and there are several across the metro. Star Kitchen's dim sum works well with children too, because the carts arrive continuously and nobody has to wait for a kitchen ticket.

When is the best time of year for brunch in Denver?

September and October. Patio brunch also stays viable well into October given Denver's sunshine.
