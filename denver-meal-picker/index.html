    How it works

    What the picker does, and what it will not pretend to know

    The picker works from one list: the 37 Denver restaurants in the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado, plus 14 kitchens and bars we added because the James Beard Foundation recognized them or because they have decades of independent press behind them. Every one has an address, at least two published sources and a "last verified" date in our open data file.

    Hard filters and soft preferences

    Craving and area are hard filters. Pick Mexican and LoHi and you will only see Mexican restaurants in LoHi and the neighborhoods next to it. Occasion is softer: a restaurant tagged for date nights moves up when you pick that occasion, but a strong match on everything else can still appear. Within each group, starred and Bib Gourmand restaurants sort ahead of recommended ones, because an inspector has eaten there more recently than any of us.

    The value setting reverses part of that logic. It pushes Bib Gourmands and casual, award-recognized places up and moves the long tasting menus down. It is the closest we can get to a budget filter without publishing prices we cannot keep current.

    Occasion tags are ours, and the rules are public

    Whether a restaurant suits a date or a group of eight is a judgment call, so we label those tags as our own and assign them by rule rather than by feel. Starred kitchens and tasting menus count as celebrations. Bib Gourmands and shareable cuisines count as group-friendly. Sushi counters, ramen bars, bagel shops and cocktail bars count as good for one. The full rule set is on the editorial policy page.

    Diet filters only use confirmed facts

    Only four restaurants on our list have a vegetarian or vegan option documented by the restaurant or a named publication: Beckon, Mister Oso, La Diabla Pozole y Mezcal and Ash'Kara. The vegetarian setting therefore returns only those. Many other kitchens will cook for you if asked; tell them when you book.

    Making it an evening

    When "Make it an evening" is on, the picker looks for a cocktail bar in the same part of town as your first result. The bars come from the same sourced list, so the suggestion stays short and honest: Lady Jane in LoHi, Yacht Club in Cole, and the agave list at Mezcaleria Alma.

    What it does not know

    It does not know opening hours, wait times or whether a table is free tonight, and it never will guess. Check the restaurant's own site before you go, and see our reservations guide for booking windows at the counter restaurants. Restaurants close and change hands, so if something here is out of date, tell us and we will check it.

    Common questions

    Questions, Answered

    How does the Denver meal picker choose restaurants?

    It filters our list of 51 sourced Denver restaurants by the cravings and area you pick, then sorts what is left by how well each place fits the occasion, with recognized kitchens ranked higher. It never uses ratings, reviews or paid placement.

    Does the picker know if a restaurant is open tonight?

    No. We do not publish hours because they change too often to keep accurate. Every result links to the restaurant or its MICHELIN listing so you can check hours and book.

    Why can I not filter by price?

    We only filter on facts we can source. Menu prices are not something we track, so the closest honest option is the value setting, which favours Bib Gourmands and casual, award-recognized places.

    Where do the date-night and group labels come from?

    They are our own editor’s tags, assigned by simple published rules (for example, every starred or tasting-menu restaurant counts as a celebration pick). The rules are on our editorial policy page.

    Can I share my results?

    Yes. Your choices are saved in the page address, so copying the link from your browser shares the same picks.
