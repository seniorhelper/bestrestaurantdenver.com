Denver Restaurants
By Neighborhood

Where the good food actually clusters, which neighborhoods you can walk between, and what each one is genuinely good at — ranked by Michelin recognition rather than by opinion.

Which Denver neighborhood is best for restaurants?

RiNo has the most Michelin-recognized restaurants of any Denver neighborhood, ten in the 2026 selection, from the one-star counters at Beckon and Milpero to Bib Gourmands Hop Alley and Mister Oso. LoHi is next with five, including two one-star restaurants from chef Johnny Curiel on the same block of 15th Street. For the two-star, go to Sunnyside.

Denver is not a walking city overall. However, it is a city of walkable pockets, and choosing the right pocket matters more here than choosing the right restaurant.

Because the distances are real. RiNo to Cherry Creek is a twenty-minute drive and a completely different evening. Platt Park to Berkeley is nearly half an hour. So if you are visiting for two or three nights, pick two neighborhoods and stay in them.

Below: every Denver neighborhood with a Michelin-recognized restaurant, ranked by how many it has. Then profiles of the ones worth building a night around.

In depth

The Six Worth Building
A Night Around

These are the neighborhoods where you can park once, walk, and still have options if your first choice is full.

RiNo

Most recognized restaurants

River North Art District · Larimer & Brighton corridors · ZIP 80205

RiNo has more Michelin-recognized restaurants than any other Denver neighborhood, and it earned that the hard way. Twenty years ago this was warehouses and rail spurs. Now it is a designated arts district with murals on nearly every wall and a dining core running up Larimer Street.

The concentration is what makes it useful. Beckon holds a Michelin star with an eighteen-seat chef's counter, and in 2026 Milpero on Ringsby Court became Denver's newest starred restaurant. Hop Alley on Larimer holds a Bib Gourmand for modern Chinese, and its bar director took Michelin's Exceptional Cocktails Award for all of Colorado in 2025. Mister Oso carries a Bib for pan-Latin food a few blocks away. Then there is Dio Mio for handmade pasta, Safta, Smok and Temaki Den in the Source hotel complex, and two 2026 arrivals: Dear Emilia for Emilia-Romagna cooking and Heretík, whose wine team won Michelin's 2026 Sommelier Award.

That Brighton cluster is a genuine advantage. Three recognized restaurants share essentially one address block, so if one is booked you walk twenty steps.

Meanwhile, Denver Central Market anchors the Larimer corridor as a food hall, which is a good landing spot when your group cannot agree. Several blocks of Larimer have been converted to pedestrian-friendly zones in recent years, so the walking is genuinely pleasant now rather than merely possible.

One caution. RiNo is busy, loud and heavily weekend-driven. Therefore a Tuesday here is a different and frequently better experience than a Saturday.

One night in RiNo

Cocktails at Hop Alley, dinner at Beckon if you booked a month out, Dio Mio if you did not. Walk the murals between courses. If everything is full, the Brighton Boulevard block has three recognized rooms within sight of each other.

LoHi

Two Michelin stars, one block

Lower Highlands · West of I-25 · 15th & 32nd Avenue corridors

LoHi is where Denver's most remarkable dining story is happening, and it is happening on a single stretch of 15th Street.

Chef Johnny Curiel runs Alma Fonda Fina at 2556 15th, which earned Colorado's first Michelin star for Mexican cooking. Then he opened Mezcaleria Alma immediately next door at 2550, and that earned its own star in 2025. Two starred restaurants, one chef, adjacent doors. That does not happen in most cities at all.

The neighborhood does not stop there. Ash'Kara holds a Bib Gourmand for contemporary Israeli cooking on West 33rd. Kawa Ni, an izakaya on West 32nd, is recommended. Wildflower is Michelin-recommended too, and its chef Aiden Tibbets was named Michelin's Young Chef of the Year for Colorado in 2025.

Geographically LoHi sits just across the South Platte from downtown. A pedestrian bridge connects it to LoDo in about a ten-minute walk, with Confluence Park in between. As a result, you can stay downtown and eat in LoHi without a car, which is not true of most Denver neighborhoods.

LoHi also has the views. It sits slightly above the river, so several rooms and rooftops look back across the water at the skyline.

One night in LoHi

Start with mezcal at Mezcaleria Alma, then walk six doors to Alma Fonda Fina. If both are booked, Ash'Kara on 33rd is a Bib Gourmand and considerably easier to get into. Walk over the bridge afterward for a view of downtown.

LoDo & Larimer Square

Where Denver started

Lower Downtown · 26 square blocks · Denver's first historic district

LoDo is the original city. In 1858 General William Larimer drove cottonwood stakes into the dirt beside the South Platte and claimed a square mile. That claim is now Lower Downtown.

The neighborhood went boom town, then skid row, then a district of boarded-up warehouses nobody wanted. Preservation saved it. Larimer Square, built in the late 1800s as Denver's first commercial block, became the city's first official historic district in 1971 — largely through the work of preservationist Dana Crawford. Today LoDo holds the largest concentration of restored turn-of-the-century and Victorian brick warehouses in the country, and more than 100 restaurants, breweries and bars.

For our purposes, three rooms carry Michelin recognition. Brutø at 1801 Blake in the Dairy Block holds a star and a Green Star, cooking almost everything over a central wood-fired hearth. A5 Steakhouse and Ukiyo, an omakase counter on Larimer Square, are both recommended.

Larimer Square itself is worth walking regardless. It is pedestrian-only, lined with Victorian facades, and it has been the address for James Beard-recognized Denver chefs for two decades — Jennifer Jasinski's Rioja opened there in 2004 and is still running.

LoDo does get rowdy after dark, especially near Coors Field on game nights. Accordingly, if you want quiet, eat early or head one block off the main strip.

One night in LoDo

Walk Larimer Square before dinner while it is still light and the brickwork shows. Then Brutø at the Dairy Block if you booked ahead. Union Station is a five-minute walk for a drink in the great hall afterward.

Union Station

Best arrival in the city

Wynkoop & 16th · Adjacent to LoDo · A Line to the airport

Technically this is part of LoDo. Practically it functions as its own thing, because the restored station building changed the whole block.

Two recognized restaurants sit essentially on the platform. Tavernetta at 1889 16th holds a Bib Gourmand for Italian cooking and has for multiple years running. Mercantile at 1701 Wynkoop is Michelin-recommended, and its chef Alex Seidel has built a reputation on Colorado sourcing — including turning Rocky Ford melon into a savoury gazpacho in high summer.

The practical argument for this neighborhood is transport. The A Line runs from Denver International Airport directly into Union Station in roughly 37 minutes, every 15 to 30 minutes. So you can land, take a train, and be eating within an hour of baggage claim without ever renting a car.

The great hall itself is free to sit in. Morning coffee at a table under that ceiling is one of the genuinely pleasant experiences in Denver, and it costs the price of a coffee.

One night at Union Station

Tavernetta for dinner, then a drink in the great hall. If you are arriving or departing by plane, this is the neighborhood to book — the train makes a car unnecessary for the entire trip.

Platt Park

Two stars in one building

South Pearl Street · South of downtown · Residential

This is the strangest entry on the list, and the most interesting.

Platt Park is a quiet residential neighborhood well south of downtown. It has a farmers market, a walkable stretch of South Pearl Street, and until recently no particular claim on Denver's fine dining conversation.

Then one building at 1551 S. Pearl Street produced two Michelin stars in a single year. Kizaki is an intimate Edomae sushi counter from chef Toshi Kizaki, who has shaped Denver sushi for decades and earned a first star in his seventies. Margot, in the same building, opened in June 2025 and was starred within months.

Margot also runs a bar and dining room at the front of the space serving cocktails and à la carte plates. That matters, because it means you can experience that kitchen without committing to a full tasting menu or winning a reservation lottery.

Sushi Den, Toshi Kizaki's older restaurant, sits just up the same street and has been a Denver institution for decades.

One night in Platt Park

Book the counter at Kizaki or Margot weeks out. If you cannot, take a seat at Margot's front bar and order à la carte. Walk South Pearl either side of dinner. This is a neighborhood night, not a downtown one.

Sunnyside

Colorado's only two-star

Tejon & Lowell · North of the Highlands · Quiet residential

Sunnyside holds the most decorated restaurant in the state, and nothing else on this list.

The Wolf's Tailor at 4058 Tejon Street is Colorado's first and only two-Michelin-star restaurant. It also holds a Michelin Green Star for sustainability, which it has now done three years running. Chef Kelly Whitaker builds eclectic multi-course tasting menus around Colorado ingredients, and describes grain rather than luxury protein as the restaurant's centrepiece.

The Ginger Pig, a Bib Gourmand for Asian cooking with a genuine cult following, is a few minutes west on Lowell Boulevard, which by address puts it in Berkeley rather than Sunnyside.

Otherwise Sunnyside is houses. It is a residential grid north of the Highlands with no dining strip to speak of. Consequently you come here for one specific meal and then you leave, which is fine — the meal justifies the trip.

One night in Sunnyside

The Wolf's Tailor, booked well in advance, and nothing else. Do not plan drinks nearby afterward. Plan a rideshare instead, because the tasting menu runs long and this is not a walking neighborhood at night.

Practical

Getting Between Them

Can you walk between Denver's restaurant neighborhoods?

Some of them. LoDo, Union Station and Larimer Square are all walkable to each other within about ten minutes. LoHi connects to LoDo by pedestrian bridge across the South Platte, also roughly ten minutes. RiNo is walkable within itself but a rideshare from downtown. Platt Park, Cherry Creek, Berkeley and Sunnyside all require a car or a rideshare.

Here is the honest geography, because guidebooks routinely undersell the distances.

The walkable core

Union Station, LoDo and Larimer Square form one continuous district. You can walk the whole thing in fifteen minutes. Add LoHi across the pedestrian bridge and you have four neighborhoods on foot, which is the largest walkable dining footprint in the city.

RiNo is close but not adjacent

RiNo starts roughly a mile northeast of Union Station. Walkable in daylight if you like walking, though the route is not scenic. Most people take a rideshare, and it is a short one. Once you are in RiNo, however, everything is on foot.

Everything else needs wheels

Platt Park is about fifteen minutes south by car. Cherry Creek is fifteen minutes southeast and a completely different atmosphere — quieter, more polished, more expensive. Berkeley and Sunnyside sit northwest. Washington Park is south.

None of these are far in absolute terms. Still, they are separate evenings, not stops on the same evening.

Parking, briefly

RiNo and LoHi street parking is genuinely difficult on weekend nights. LoDo has garages but they fill on Rockies game nights. Platt Park, Berkeley and Washington Park are residential and usually fine. Therefore, if you are driving, RiNo on a Saturday is the one to plan around.

The airport question

The A Line runs from Denver International Airport to Union Station in about 37 minutes, every 15 to 30 minutes. So if your trip is downtown-focused, you can skip the rental car entirely and rideshare the two or three times you leave the core.

Common questions

Denver Dining, Answered

Which Denver neighborhood is best for restaurants?

RiNo has the most Michelin-recognized restaurants of any Denver neighborhood, ten in the 2026 selection, from the one-star counters at Beckon and Milpero to Bib Gourmands Hop Alley and Mister Oso. LoHi is next with five, including two one-star restaurants from chef Johnny Curiel on the same block of 15th Street. For the two-star, go to Sunnyside.

What is RiNo in Denver?

RiNo is the River North Art District, a former warehouse and rail area northeast of downtown that is now Denver's densest dining neighborhood. It holds more Michelin-recognized restaurants than any other part of the city, including Beckon, Hop Alley, Dio Mio, Safta, Smok and Temaki Den. The Larimer and Brighton corridors are the two main strips.

What does LoHi stand for?

LoHi is short for Lower Highlands, the neighborhood just west of downtown Denver across the South Platte River. It holds two Michelin-starred restaurants on adjacent doors of 15th Street — Alma Fonda Fina and Mezcaleria Alma, both from chef Johnny Curiel — plus Bib Gourmands at Ash'Kara and Mister Oso.

Can you walk between Denver's restaurant neighborhoods?

Some of them. Union Station, LoDo and Larimer Square form one continuous walkable district of about fifteen minutes end to end. LoHi connects to LoDo by pedestrian bridge across the South Platte, roughly ten minutes. RiNo is walkable within itself but is about a mile from Union Station, so most people rideshare. Platt Park, Cherry Creek, Berkeley and Sunnyside all require a car.

Is LoDo worth visiting for food?

Yes, though more for the setting than the density of awards. LoDo holds the largest concentration of restored turn-of-the-century and Victorian brick warehouses in the country and more than 100 restaurants, breweries and bars. Three carry Michelin recognition — Brutø in the Dairy Block, plus A5 Steakhouse and Ukiyo. Larimer Square, the city's first historic district, is worth walking regardless.

Where should I stay in Denver for food?

Downtown near Union Station or LoDo, if you want to eat without a car. From there LoDo, Larimer Square and Union Station are on foot, LoHi is a ten-minute walk over the pedestrian bridge, and RiNo is a short rideshare. The A Line also runs from the airport into Union Station in about 37 minutes, so you can skip the rental car entirely.

What neighborhood has Denver's two-Michelin-star restaurant?

Sunnyside. The Wolf's Tailor at 4058 Tejon Street is Colorado's first and only two-star restaurant, and it held a Michelin Green Star for sustainability in the 2023 through 2025 selections. Sunnyside is otherwise a quiet residential grid north of the Highlands; The Ginger Pig, a Bib Gourmand, is a few minutes west in Berkeley.

Is parking hard in Denver's restaurant neighborhoods?

In RiNo and LoHi on weekend nights, yes. LoDo has garages but they fill on Colorado Rockies game nights at Coors Field. Platt Park, Berkeley and Washington Park are residential and usually straightforward. If you are driving on a Saturday, RiNo is the one to plan around — or take a rideshare and skip it.

Which Denver neighborhood is best for Mexican food?

LoHi. Chef Johnny Curiel holds two Michelin stars there on adjacent addresses — Alma Fonda Fina at 2556 15th Street and Mezcaleria Alma at 2550. Ballpark also has La Diabla Pozole y Mezcal, a Bib Gourmand, and Cherry Creek has Alteño, which is Michelin-recommended.

How many Denver neighborhoods have Michelin-recognized restaurants?

Sixteen, based on the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado selection. RiNo leads with ten, followed by LoHi with five. Five Points and Cheesman Park are new to the list in 2026. Several neighborhoods, including Baker, West Highland, Cole and Larimer Square, have exactly one each, which often makes them a destination trip rather than a walking evening.
