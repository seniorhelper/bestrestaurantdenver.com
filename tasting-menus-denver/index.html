Tasting Menus &
Chef's Counters

Most of Denver's Michelin stars are small counters. How they book, what to expect, and how to choose the right one.Updated 10 October 2026 · Best Restaurant Denver editors

Which Denver restaurants serve tasting menus?

Most of Denver's starred restaurants are tasting-menu or chef's-counter rooms: The Wolf's Tailor (two stars), Beckon, Brutø, Margot, Kizaki and Milpero (one star each). Ukiyo, a recommended omakase room near Larimer Square, works the same way. These rooms seat few people, release reservations in batches and often take payment in advance.

A chef's counter is the most rewarding way to eat in Denver and the hardest to book. Here is how the city's counters work and how to choose between them.

Denver's top end is unusually counter-heavy. Instead of large dining rooms, most of its starred kitchens serve a set menu to a small number of guests who watch it being cooked. That shapes everything about the experience, from how far ahead you book to what happens if you need to cancel.

How we picked these

We list the two-star first, then the one-star counters in the order they were starred, then the recommended omakase room. Consider it a reading order rather than a league table; restaurants cannot pay to appear.

How booking works

Plan like it is a ticket

Counter restaurants treat a reservation more like an event ticket than a table hold, because an empty seat at an eight- or eighteen-seat counter cannot be filled by a walk-in.

  • Find the release schedule. Many counters publish dates in batches at a set time. The restaurant's own website or booking page will say when.
  • Expect to pay up front. Prepayment or a card hold with a cancellation window is common. Read the policy before you confirm; we do not list prices, which change with the menu.
  • Declare restrictions at booking. Set menus are planned in advance. Some counters can adapt with notice; some, like Milpero by its own listing, cannot accommodate many restrictions.
  • Use waitlists. Notify alerts on booking platforms are the best way to catch cancellations, which go quickly.
  • Arrive on time. A counter usually serves everyone together. Arriving late can mean missing courses.
  • Cancel early if plans change. A no-show at a tiny counter is a real loss for a small business.

How to choose

For the single most decorated meal in Colorado, The Wolf's Tailor. For the most theatrical counter, Beckon or Brutø's hearth. For sushi, Kizaki, or Ukiyo if you want an easier booking. For the newest star, Milpero. For a first taste without a full commitment, Margot's bar.

On the night

What a counter evening is like

A tasting menu is a set sequence of small courses chosen by the kitchen, usually served to everyone at the same pace. At a counter you sit facing the cooks, so the meal doubles as a view of the kitchen at work, and chefs often present dishes themselves. Expect the evening to take a few hours, and plan transport home accordingly.

Drinks are usually offered as an optional pairing or by the glass and bottle. If you are visiting from sea level, remember that Denver sits at 5,280 feet and alcohol can hit harder than at home; a lighter pairing or a non-alcoholic option, which many counters offer, is a sensible choice on your first night in town.

Photography, dress and celebrations vary by restaurant. When in doubt, ask when you book: counters are small, and the staff would rather plan for a birthday or a quiet table than be surprised by one.

Common questions

Questions, Answered

How far ahead should I book a tasting menu in Denver?

As soon as the restaurant releases dates. Several counters publish reservations in batches and fill quickly; check the restaurant's own website for its release schedule and set an alert.

Which Denver restaurants have chef's counters?

Beckon, Brutø, Kizaki and Margot all seat guests at a counter, and Alma Fonda Fina has a chef's counter alongside its dining room. Ukiyo is a small omakase room.

Do Denver tasting-menu restaurants take walk-ins?

Rarely at the counter itself. Margot's bar serves à la carte, and some restaurants keep bar seats for walk-ins; check each restaurant's policy.
